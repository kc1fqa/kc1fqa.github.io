Athena Pro Line

Mom / veg: Pro Grow · Bloom 1: Pro Bloom

Dry soluble salts, mixed into liquid concentrates and then dosed into the reservoir to a target EC. Built for drip and fertigation; Athena's run is 2–3 weeks veg and 8–9 weeks flower.

ProductRole
Pro GrowVeg base. Nitrogen for vegetative growth, no urea or ammoniacal N.
Pro CoreCalcium nitrate base used through every stage, alongside Grow or Bloom.
Pro BloomFlower base: phosphorus, potassium (as sulfate) and micros.
Pro FadeReplaces Pro Core for the last 2 weeks of flower to take nitrogen out.
Proper New · soon for home growersPeak-flower supplement for Pro and Blended Line, flower weeks 4–8. It partly replaces the base nutrients rather than adding on top, raising phosphorus and potassium while stepping nitrogen down as the crop matures.
Pro BalancePotassium carbonate pH up; buffers RO water. Not a nutrient.

Mixing

  • Concentrate strength: 2 lb per gallon (imperial). Metric charts assume 2 lb per 4 L, a different dilution, so use the matching chart.
  • Making a concentrate: fill the container to 80% of final volume, empty the whole pouch in, top up to final volume, then mix until fully dissolved. Always use a full pouch.
  • Check it: weigh 1000 g of RO water, add the chart's amount of concentrate and read EC with a calibrated meter before mixing a full batch.
  • Mixed at a different strength? Adjust the reservoir dose to hit the chart's EC.
  • With Proper: it goes into the water first in the mixing order, before the other concentrates.

Notes from the chart

Jack's 321

Not assigned to a tent

Three dry salts mixed straight into the reservoir: Part A (5-12-26), Part B calcium nitrate (15-0-0) and Epsom salt. The name comes from the roughly 3 : 2 : 1 ratio.

Mixing

  • Per-gallon amounts are Jack's per-100-gallon figures divided by 100. Many growers round to 3.6 g A, 2.4 g B, 1.2 g Epsom.
  • Dissolve Part A fully first, then Epsom, then Part B. Never add dry calcium nitrate straight into Part A solution: it can drop out as calcium sulfate.
  • Finish with a 4–7 day clear-water flush.

Roots Organics Terp Tea

Not assigned to a tent

Organic dry nutrients: Grow for veg and Bloom for flower, with Bloom Booster for late flower and Microbe Charge for root-zone biology. Each can be brewed as a tea, fed directly, or top dressed.

ProductNPKLiquid / teaTop dress
Grow7-1-11 tsp–1 Tbsp/gal1 tsp/gal of mix
Bloom3-7-41 tsp–1 Tbsp/gal1 tsp/gal of mix
Bloom Booster0.5-6-181 Tbsp/gal1 Tbsp/gal of mix
Microbe Charge2-1-21 tsp–1 Tbsp/gal1 tsp/gal of mix

Mixing

  • Brew 24 hours in an aerated, well-ventilated spot, or feed directly.
  • For a big reservoir, mix it up in a 5-gallon bucket first, then add it.
  • Bloom Booster: a late-flower supplement, mostly potassium with some phosphorus, plus beneficial bacteria. It's the one product in the line dosed at a full tablespoon.
  • Microbe Charge: worm castings, kelp, guano and other organics, used as a base amendment to boost microbial activity, as part of the regular feed, or as a spot supplement. Brewing it into a tea is the preferred way to use it.
  • Grow adds 3% humic acid, and the line carries Bacillus species and mycorrhizal fungi, so skip harsh sterilizers in the same water.

Spraying basics

  • Spray with the lights off or in early morning, and let plants dry before lights come back on.
  • Contact sprays only work where they land: soak the tops and undersides of every leaf and the stems.
  • Use RO water to keep residue off the leaves, and test a new mix on one plant first.
  • Rotate products rather than leaning on one, and keep oils and heavy sprays off flowers.

Athena IPM + Stack

Pairs with the Athena feed program

IPM is a plant-oil contact spray that kills soft-bodied insects and mildew on contact. Stack is a kelp foliar (0-0-1) that tank-mixes with it.

UsePer gallonHow often
IPM, prevention (label)50–60 mL2–3× a week
IPM, feed-chart preventative90 mL2× a week
IPM, treatment90–120 mL3× a week; at least 3 sprays 3 days apart
IPM, root drench5–10 mLSaturate media to slight runoff
Stack7 mL1–2× a week
  • Active ingredients (IPM): peppermint, lemongrass and geranium oils (1.5% each) and citric acid.
  • Window: Athena's spray program runs veg weeks 1–4 and flower weeks 1–3; Stack stops after flower week 3.
  • Tank mix: fill with RO water, add IPM and mix, then add Stack. Give it 3–4 hours to dry before high-intensity lights come on.
  • Start at the low end of the treatment range and step up if the problem holds on. Wear PPE when spraying.

Lost Coast Plant Therapy

Peppermint oil and citric acid in a soap base. It suffocates and dehydrates soft-bodied insects, their larvae and eggs, and washes off powdery mildew, mold and fungus. It has to touch the pest to work.

UsePer gallonHow often
Standard spray1 oz (2 Tbsp)Repeat as needed
Russet mites, hemp aphids2 ozDaily for a week, then as needed
Maintenance / prevention1 ozOnce a week once pests are under control
Soil drench1 ozDaily for 2–5 days, then as needed
  • Shake the concentrate before measuring, and shake the sprayer again before and during use.
  • Saturate the whole plant, especially the leaf undersides and stems.
  • Try the soil drench on one plant first: some cultivars are more sensitive.

The Amazing Doctor Zymes Eliminator

Organic materials and citric acid that attack pests and mold several ways at once, including shifting the leaf surface pH so new growth can't take hold. Works best before an infestation gets bad.

UseRateHow often
Standard / large volume4 oz (½ cup) per gal warm waterMites, thrips, aphids, gnats: 3 days in a row, then weekly
Severe infestation½ cup per galEvery 3rd day until clear
Strong spot spray2–2.5 oz per 30 oz warm waterRinse after an hour to avoid leaf burn
  • Mix with warm water and don't add a wetting agent.
  • Spray tops and undersides of leaves, stems, stalks and the base of the plant. Avoid spraying beneficial insects.

Neem oil

Broad-spectrum insecticide, miticide and fungicide from the neem tree. Rates below are for a 70% clarified hydrophobic neem concentrate (Monterey-type). Cold-pressed products like Captain Jack's NeemMax use different rates, so check your bottle.

UsePer gallonHow often
Fungal disease (powdery mildew)2 Tbsp (1 fl oz)Every 7–14 days
Insects and mites2–4 Tbsp (1–2 fl oz)Every 7–14 days; every 7 for heavy pressure
  • Keep the spray agitated while you work: the oil separates.
  • Toxic to bees, and it leaves an oily residue: keep it to veg and off flowers.

Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)

A bacterium (subspecies kurstaki) that only kills caterpillars that eat it, such as loopers, hornworms and budworms. No effect on bees, ladybugs, earthworms or birds when used as directed.

ProductPer gallonHow often
Bonide Thuricide½–4 tsp (label varies by crop)Every 7–10 days while caterpillars are active
Monterey B.t.4 tspAt first sign, then weekly as needed
  • Spray leaf surfaces top and bottom; caterpillars have to eat it. Use the mix within 12–24 hours.
  • Thuricide can be applied up to the day of harvest. Don't run it through an irrigation system.
